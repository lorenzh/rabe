# Focus order and arming

#### The held order

If rows reorder while a row has the focus, the focus ring stays on the old index, so Enter presses another row (see [the feasibility limits](../feasibility/ui.md)). So the lists do not reorder while the pane is open. `/rabe` stores `orderOf(items)` in `rabe.order`: the item ids of each Items group sorted (running first, then the newest), the Cost list by tokens, the Timeline by start and the Effects files by `byConflict` (as `file:<path>`). Each list then goes through `stable(list, held, sort)`: the held ids first in held order, then the items Rabe saw since, in the order it saw them. On the Items tab a held item keeps its group when its status changes, and an item Rabe saw since the open goes into the group of its kind (AGENTS, SHELLS, MONITORS or CRON), also when it failed, so it never changes group either; it comes after the held rows of that group, in the order Rabe saw it. A new shell or monitor joins the block of the agent that started it. Such a row can stand above the focused row; the renderer seats it there and the pane moves the ring back (see [Rows found later in a list](focus-order.md#rows-found-later-in-a-list)). SHELLS and MONITORS are held in their family order (`byParent`), so the blocks drawn at the open are the held order. The Effects tab holds its files and its ports (`ports`: the shells that ran with a port at the open). Its files and ports found since the open follow the held ones in FILES and PORTS, in the order they were found (a file's first edit, a port's `portAt`), seated like the Items list's new rows. Opening the pane again sorts anew. Without `rabe.order` (nothing opened the pane yet) the lists are sorted on each draw. The held order is for reading: the rows stay where the person saw them. The focus is safe without it, since the renderer holds the focus order (see The append-only focus order).

#### The append-only focus order

The engine keeps the focus ring on an index in document order, not on a key, and `$.ui.focus` on the old key brings it back only when called after the new tree is drawn (see [the feasibility limits](../feasibility/ui.md)). So any Button, Input or Select that comes, goes or moves above the focused one while the pane holds the keys puts the ring on another element, and Enter acts on that one: it could stop the wrong shell. Fixing this per view missed case after case (NEW rows passing the controls, a workflow phase found late, the edit history cut at 100, a tab cut off at a narrow width), so the rule lives in the renderer, where no view can break it: **while the pane is open, the sequence of focusable keys it draws only grows at the end.** The one extension is the Items list and the Effects tab, whose new rows stand in their group or section and whose gone rows leave; the pane then puts the ring back on its element (see [Rows found later in a list](focus-order.md#rows-found-later-in-a-list)).

- `hold(pieces, held)` in `render.tsx` takes the drawing in document order (`layout(drawn)`) and the keys drawn before (`Held`: key, latest label, control or row). It draws the held keys first, in their order: each element in place, or brought forward to its turn when the view moved it, or, when it is gone, a slot with the same key and its old label, dim, whose press does nothing (a row reads `gone name`, the word first so a cut keeps it; a control `[ x: stop ]` dim). A slot follows the element held before it, so it never stands under the heading of the next one (an Effects port's `:3000  serve` line). Then come the keys that are new, in document order: one drawn above a held one moves to just after the last held one; one already below stays. A line with several Buttons (the tab row) moves as one, so its keys never change.
- The layout makes this rare: the toolbar sits above the body, so rows found later end the order without passing a control; a control that cannot act stays as a dim slot; the tabs never cut a tab; the views keep their held list order for the reader.
- The [interaction model](interaction.md) keeps the held keys per surface in a module value: a render hook may not write `$.state` (drawing is pure, and the host refuses the write), and the scroll hook reads them to step the arrows in the drawn order. The hold is for one view, its `scope`: the tab, the open item, the search, the folded groups and the Timeline window's hours. A change of these is the person's own, so the hold starts anew, and `act` moves the ring to a safe element (see [Focus and the arrow keys](focus-and-keys.md#focus-and-the-arrow-keys)). An open item that is pruned keeps the scope: the view falls back to the list, and the detail's keys stay as slots above it. `/rabe` clears the hold, and so does a close of the pane: the `ui.close` hook passes the close on and, when `$.ui.panes()` no longer lists the pane, feeds `closed`, so a close the host cancels keeps the hold. A reload of the hooks module empties it without telling the engine, which keeps the ring's index.
- `render.test.tsx` checks the rule with a seeded random walk on every tab and on the detail of a workflow, an agent, a shell, a cron job and a Codex job, on the terminal and the desktop: items come and go, change status and phase, edits leave the history, ports are found, the width and the selected row change, and after each step the keys drawn before are a prefix of the keys drawn now; on the Items list and the Effects tab they keep their order, only rows and group headers come between them or leave, and every tab and control keeps its index. Between steps the walk also presses a tab, a row, `b`, a group header or the search like the person: the view starts its hold anew, the first key of `landing` is drawn, and the element the ring lands on neither stops nor deletes. After each step the walk also checks what every focusable element acts on (its target: the items a stop stops, the item a delete or message acts on, the text a copy copies): a key never changes its target, and an index of the hold never acts on another target than it did before; the list's `stop` and `stop-group` may change theirs only on the person's focus on a live row, else the walk's arming, fed as `pane.tsx` feeds it, must hold them inert. The walk's person also focuses gone slots and group headers: such a focus never arms `x` and `g`, and while the selected row is gone they stay inert. The person also presses arrows that Rabe carries out (`step`, then a plugin's focus and `landed`), some refused, some onto a row that went meanwhile, some that a hook beneath sends onto another row or header and some that a hook beneath swallows (answers `{}` without `next`), as it does some of the person's own focus moves. The walk reads each move through `landingOf` from a trace that ends at the engine or at that hook: the selection follows the landing, `x` and `g` arm exactly when the move lands, as asked, on a live row, and a refused or swallowed move keeps the selection and disarms the pane. Its presses on another live row of the list select it and arm `x` and `g` for it, as a click does. A test walks every row of the list and finds one `x` and one `g`. A test checks that the first element after the tabs is no stop in any view. The four cases above have their own tests.

#### Rows found later in a list

A new item stands in the group of its kind (see [The held order](focus-order.md#the-held-order)), so its row, and the head of a group that was empty, can come above the focused row. With `inPlace` (`seatsRows`: the Items list with no open item, and the Effects tab, whose new files and ports stand in FILES and PORTS) `hold` seats each new line that holds one row or group header Button where the view draws it: after the held key before it and the gone slots that follow that key. It never seats one above a held tab, control or Input, so these keep their index; a new control still goes to the end. The same views drop a gone row or group header instead of drawing its slot (issue #122), but only below the last held tab, control or Input, so no tab or control changes its index; a gone row above one keeps its slot. The engine keeps the ring on its index, so a seated row above it, or a dropped row above it, moves the ring onto another row (see [the feasibility limits](../feasibility/ui.md)):

- The interaction model compares the held keys before and after the drawing (`shifts`): when the element the ring sits on (`ringOn`) stands at another index now, it keeps that key in `shift`.
- While `shift` is set and the pane holds the keys, a press on a row or group header does nothing (`isHeldBack`): Enter would press the row that took the ring's index (on the Effects tab, copy another `ssh -L` line). A press while the pane does not hold the keys is a click, which presses what the person points at, so it acts. Hotkeys act on the selection, which no seated row changes, so they act as before.
- When the ring's own row was dropped, there is no key to put the ring back on: `shift` keeps the gone key and the pane asks no regain, so row and group header presses stay held back until the person moves the ring (Tab, an arrow, a click that moves it). The selected row is gone too, so `x`, `g` and `r` stay inert (see [Arming](focus-order.md#arming)).
- 100 ms after such a drawing, while the pane holds the keys, `regain` calls `$.ui.focus` on that key. A call made while the hook draws resolves against the tree before the one it returns, where the key still stands at its old index, so the ring stayed off its element (tested live). From the timer the call reaches no hook of Rabe's, so the engine's answer is the evidence: `{}` clears `shift`; a refusal (for example `the move was abandoned`, when another drawing came first) counts as a refused landing and disarms, and the next drawing asks again, at most three times, and three times more each time the pane takes the keys back. Rows that come and go above the ring move it again, but while `shift` stays on the same key the count goes on, so they never reset it.
- A landing the pane's `ui.focus` hook sees (the person's Tab or arrow, Rabe's own moves) clears `shift`, and a pending `regain` then does nothing, so it never undoes the person's move. A reset clears it too.
- `shift` is a module value, like the hold: a reload drops it, and the reload already starts the pane disarmed.

`hold` with `inPlace` keeps the rule that matters for safety: no tab or control changes its index, and a key never changes its target; only rows and group headers, which open, select, fold or copy, move, come or leave, and while one may have moved under the ring the pane holds their presses back. Elsewhere (the Cost and Timeline tabs, an open item) new rows still go to the end and gone rows keep their slots until the view changes or the pane closes.

The hold alone does not make Enter safe: it keeps keys in place, and a key whose action follows the view, or a hold the pane starts anew while the engine keeps the ring's index, still lets Enter act on a target the person did not choose. Review found four such cases: an open item or a selected row that is gone, so the view falls back and a generic `stop` key keeps its slot with a new target; a landing another hook refuses while the pane keeps the keys; a reload that empties the hold; and, after such a fallback, a focus on the group header and back on the gone slot of the selected row, which armed `x` on the row the list fell back to, a row the person never chose and may not see. The rules: the keys of a detail name their target; the list's `x` and `g` act on the selection and arm only on the person's focus on a live row, so a selection that resolves through a fallback leaves them inert; arming covers what changes without the person. The random walk checks these rules on the views it reaches; it does not prove that no other case exists.

#### Target-bound keys

In a detail, every control that acts on an item carries its target in its key: `stop:<item id>` (`x`), `stop-run:<run id>` (`g` on a workflow run), `delete:<item id>`, `copy:<item id>` (a command, a prompt, an agent's id), `forwarder:<agent item id>` (opens the agent folded into a Codex job), `message-agent:<item id>` and the Input `message:<item id>`. A control that copies a resume command is keyed by the id it resumes, `resume:<id>` (a Codex thread in its job's detail, this session on the Cost tab, the previous session on the Timeline tab): a Codex job learns its thread after it started, so the key appears then, at the end of the focus order, and never changes what it copies. There the target changes only without the person (the open item ends or is pruned), so a new target is a new key: `hold` draws the old key as an inert gone slot and puts the new control at the end of the focus order. A slot that existed before never acts on another item.

The list's toolbar is the exception: `x: stop` keeps the key `stop`, `g` (`g: stop group` or `g: stop run`) the key `stop-group`, `r: remove` the key `remove` and, while the detail shows beside the list, `d: delete job` the key `delete`, and all four act on the current selection (`LIST_KEYS`). Beside the detail the list also has `c` (key `copy`) and `m` (key `message`); they act on the selection too, but copy and message change nothing by themselves, so they need no arming and the walk in `render.test.tsx` does not hold their targets. After a send, the ring goes back to the list's `m` (`messageLanding`). The person moves the selection on purpose with the arrows, Tab or a click, and a key per target would leave a dim slot in the toolbar for every row walked past. They arm only on the person's focus on a live row (see [Arming](focus-order.md#arming)); `targetsOf` counts a delete's job like a stop's ids. A target they gain without the person (the selected row or search result is gone and the list falls back to its first row, a row joins the group) disarms the pane; a row that leaves the group (it ended) only makes `g` stop less than the person saw, and keeps it armed, also when `g` can no longer act (`targetsOf` lists no target for a control without action).

The group-stop label includes the number of stoppable rows. Its key remains `stop-group` as the count changes, so the label update does not move focus.

Row Buttons stay keyed by what they show (`row:<item id>`, `row:file:<path>`): Enter on a row opens or copies and stops nothing. An Effects file row opens the agent that edited it last, which may change under the same key.

#### Arming

Destructive controls (stop, stop group, stop run, delete, remove, remove ended) act only while the pane is armed: the ring is known to sit on a safe element. Disarmed, `paneView` draws them dim, with the action `NONE` and their hotkeys still bound (`Selection.isArmed`), and the hint leaves out `x`, `g`, `r` and `a`. The logic is pure (`arm(state, event)` in `view.ts`); the interaction model holds the arming state and feeds it the events, and redraws when it flips:

- Disarm on `/rabe` (the open), on every change of the view through `act` (tab, open, back, fold, search: each has a `landing`), on a drawing whose open item or selected Items row is gone and the view falls back (`fallbackOf` in `views/pane.ts`, `open:<id>`, `selected:<id>` or both; once per new fallback), on a drawing whose list `x` or `g` gains a target (`targetsOf` in `view.ts`: one entry per control and item) while the stored selection `rabe.selected`, which only the person changes, stayed the same (the render hook draws the view armed to learn the targets, then again disarmed if it must; the first drawing after a reset takes its fallback and targets as given, since the landing that follows accounts for them), and on a `$.ui.focus` of Rabe's that is refused (`{ deny }`), throws or lands on another element than it asked for: `land`, the arrow hook and the `s` and `m` controls all go through one `focusOn`.
- A reload of the hooks module starts disarmed: the state lives in module memory, which is what a reload resets, the same memory as the hold.
- The list's `x`, `g` and `r` need more (`isListArmed`): the person's focus on a row the list draws live (`isLiveRow`: not a gone slot). The person's focus on a gone slot makes them inert; a focus on a group header, a tab or a control, a landing and `autoFocus` leave them as they were, so after a reset they stay inert until the person moves onto a row. While the selected row is gone (`selected:` in the fallback) they stay inert in every drawing: the row they would act on is one the list fell back to, not one the person chose. Each reset and each disarm above also makes them inert.
- Where the ring landed: a hook beneath Rabe's may send a `ui.focus` onto another element (`next({ ...e, element })`), refuse it, or answer `{}` without `next`, which keeps the ring where it was. The pane's `ui.focus` hook reads the landing from `next.trace` with `landingOf`: only a last link of Claude Code's own (tier `core`, the engine, or `builtin`, bundled in the binary, where the test kit's own hooks stand in for the engine) whose outcome is `returned` is evidence, and its `received.element` is where the ring landed. A move without that evidence, or refused, counts as refused: the selection goes back to what it was before the move, and the pane disarms (`landed` not moved). The selection follows a landing: a row stores its id in `rabe.selected`; another element keeps the selection from before the move. The hook writes the asked row before `next`, so the drawing during the move shows it, and puts the selection right after. The `focus` event carries the asked element (`requested`), the landed one (`key`) and the stored selection. A move that lands elsewhere than asked is nobody's choice: `x` and `g` go inert, and the pane disarms when it landed on a destructive element. `x` and `g` arm only while `rabe.selected` names the landed row. `focusOn` counts Rabe's call as moved only when the hook saw it land on the asked key (`landedOn`), so a rewritten, swallowed or refused landing or arrow disarms.
- A press that selects a live row of the Items list (`{ type: 'press' }`) arms `x` and `g` for it (`isListArmed`), and Rabe moves the ring onto the row (#186): a later Enter presses that row, which opens it (see [Focus follows the person](focus-and-keys.md#focus-and-the-arrow-keys)). Only a surface raises `ui.press` (a click, Enter, a hotkey; a plugin's `$` has no press), so a press is the person's.
- Arm on a `$.ui.focus` of Rabe's that landed where it asked, and on a `ui.focus` on the pane that no later hook refused, swallowed or sent elsewhere: by the person on any element, also a stop they chose, or by Rabe on an element that is not destructive (`isDestructive`: `stop`, `stop-group`, `remove`, `clear` and the prefixes `stop:`, `stop-run:`, `delete:`).
- A focus is the person's when the engine stamps it so (`origin.kind` `person`: Tab, an arrow where all rows fit, a click that moves the ring), and also the one move Rabe makes only to carry out the person's arrow in a pane taller than its body: the arrow hook feeds `{ type: 'step', key }` before its `$.ui.focus`, and the next `ui.focus` onto that `key` counts as the person's although it arrives with origin `plugin`. The next `focus` or `landed` event clears the step, so it covers that one move: a move that lands on another element (a hook rewrote it), a row gone before the move lands (not `isLiveRow`), a refused or throwing call, and every later focus of Rabe's (the landings after a change of the view, `autoFocus`, the `s` and `m` controls) stay Rabe's and never arm `x` and `g`. A reset drops the step too.

Limits: arming knows where the ring was put, not where it is. It relies on the hold: once armed, the drawings that follow keep every held key at its index, so the ring stays on that element until a reset, a fallback, a list target the person did not choose or a reload, which all disarm. A press by click or hotkey does not need the ring and acts on the live control the person sees, but `UiPressArgument` does not tell a click from Enter or a hotkey, so a disarmed stop is inert for a click too. While the pane does not hold the keys (a click gives it none), every landing is refused: after a change of the view by click, stop and delete stay dim until the person gives the pane the keys and moves the ring. The engine's own moves that raise no `ui.focus` (a reorder of rows Rabe never does, a resize between dock and inline, which takes the keys) are not evidence either way. Disarmed controls keep their hotkeys bound but do nothing. Outside input fields, only controls the current view owns keep their hotkeys. Other letters go to the prompt because the API has no key hook to catch them.
